# Historical inspiration for the island armory

All 18 catalog entries represent weapon designs or families that existed before 1890. These are original, stylized game models. They are not dimensionally exact replicas, museum reconstructions or an assertion that these arms were present at this Norwegian cabin. Damage, prices, carried ammunition, accuracy, shot spread, projectile speed and reload durations are game balance values.

The displayed year identifies a model, patent or representative period. It does **not** uniformly mean the first year of manufacture. The generic crossbow, dueling pistol, blunderbuss and Frontier Musket deliberately combine period features. Museum object records and manufacturer histories below establish the historical design families without importing their images or meshes.

| Frontier Musket | 1777 | A fictional long flintlock using late eighteenth-century military furniture and a ramrod. [Colonial Williamsburg's Pattern 1777 Land Service Musket](https://emuseum.history.org/objects/23565/pattern-1777-land-service-musket) records a walnut stock with iron, steel and brass. This is a period reference, not a claim that the game reproduces that specimen. |
| Hammer Coach Gun | 1878 | A shortened fictional double with exposed hammers. The [Old State House Museum's Colt Model 1878](https://collections.oldstatehouse.com/objects/1556/gun-12-gauge-double-barrel-shotgun) is a side-by-side exposed-hammer shotgun. The game's shortened stock and barrel arrangement are an artistic interpretation. |
| Winchester 1873 | 1873 | Lever-operated repeating rifle/carbine family. [Winchester's historical timeline](https://www.winchesterguns.com/news/historical-timeline/1860-1899.html) records the Model 1873. The game's twelve-round capacity represents its own carbine configuration and balance. |
| Hunting Crossbow | c. 1650 | A representative seventeenth-century sporting crossbow, not a design invented in 1650. The [Cleveland Museum of Art's German crossbow](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1916.1729) is dated to the early seventeenth century. The Met explains the continued [sporting and hunting use of crossbows](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/23337) after firearms became prevalent. |
| Dueling Flintlock | c. 1790 | Single-shot decorated flintlock pistol. The Met's [John Manton dueling pistols](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/830411) date to 1790–93 and provide a period example of decorated mounts and long barrels. |
| Brass Blunderbuss | c. 1780 | A fictional brass interpretation of the bell-mouthed flintlock family. [Museums Victoria's English blunderbuss](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/items/373458) dates to about 1780; that surviving example has a steel barrel and brass furniture. The game's all-brass flared barrel is a visual choice. |
| Allen Pepperbox | 1837 | Six rotating barrels rather than a revolver's cylinder feeding one barrel. The [Smithsonian's Allen & Thurber pepperbox](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1325690) records the six-barrel percussion type. The [Royal Armouries museum shop's historical description](https://shop.royalarmouries.org/products/allen-and-thurber-pepperbox-pistol) identifies its replica's 1837 reference. |
| Colt 1851 Navy | 1851 | Six-shot cap-and-ball revolver with octagonal barrel. The [Autry Museum's 1851 specimen](https://collections.theautry.org/mwebcgi/mweb.exe?request=record%3Bid%3DM515835%3Btype%3D101) records those characteristics. “1851” is the model designation: [Smithsonian research](https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1005116) dates the family’s production from 1850. |
| Remington 1858 | 1858 | Familiar shorthand for the solid-frame Remington percussion family. The [Smithsonian's Remington-Beals Model 1858 Navy record](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_416582) uses that name. The date refers to the Beals patent tradition; later New Model variants were manufactured in the 1860s. See the primary collector study of surviving markings in [Remington Navy Revolvers](https://americansocietyofarmscollectors.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/1961-B4-Remington-Navy-Revolvers.pdf). |
| LeMat Revolver | 1856 | Nine revolving chambers around a separate central shot barrel. The [NRA Museum's LeMat record](https://www.nramuseum.org/guns/the-galleries/a-nation-asunder-1861-to-1865/case-17-civil-war-imported-arms/lemat-second-model-percussion-revolver.aspx) identifies the 1856 patent and the two ammunition paths. The game gives the central barrel its own one-shot load, reserve and selector. |
| Remington Derringer | 1866 | Compact over-under two-shot pocket pistol. The [NRA Museum's Double Derringer record](https://www.nramuseum.org/guns/the-galleries/the-american-west-1850-to-1900/case-21-tin-stars-sixguns-frontier-justice/remington-double-derringer-%283%29.aspx) dates its introduction to 1866. |
| Colt Single Action | 1873 | Six-shot cartridge revolver with a loading gate. The [National Park Service's 1873 Colt record](https://www.nps.gov/fosm/learn/historyculture/1873-colt-revolver.htm) describes its six-round capacity and individual extraction. [Colt's own timeline](https://www.colt.com/timeline/) confirms the 1873 introduction. |
| Schofield Revolver | 1875 | Top-break, single-action Smith & Wesson family. The [National Park Service's Schofield record](https://www.nps.gov/fosm/learn/historyculture/1875-schofield.htm) documents the 1875 model. Its hinged frame and simultaneous extraction distinguish the game's shorter reload from loading-gate revolvers. |
| Snider-Enfield | 1866 | Hinged-breech conversion of a muzzle-loading rifle. The [National Army Museum's service-rifle history](https://collection.nam.ac.uk/detail.php?acc=1995-01-208-1) identifies the Snider's 1866 introduction and its converted origin. |
| Martini-Henry | 1871 | Purpose-built breech-loading service rifle using a lever-operated falling block. The same [National Army Museum record](https://collection.nam.ac.uk/detail.php?acc=1995-01-208-1) dates introduction to 1871. The game emphasizes a heavy single shot and a distinct underlever. |
| Sharps 1874 | 1874 | Heavy sporting rifle with falling-block action. The [Smithsonian's Model 1874 sporting rifle](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1253086) records the type. The model name is not its first production date: the [NRA Museum's historical record](https://www.nramuseum.org/guns/the-galleries/the-american-west-1850-to-1900/case-18-hunting-and-military-arms-on-the-western-frontier/sharps-model-1874-old-reliable-single-shot-falling-block-rifle-%281%29.aspx) notes manufacture beginning in 1871. |
| Spencer Carbine | 1865 | Lever-operated seven-shot repeater with its magazine in the buttstock. The [NRA Museum's Spencer record](https://www.nramuseum.org/guns/the-galleries/a-nation-asunder-1861-to-1865/case-14-union-carbines/1860-spencer-carbine.aspx) explains the seven-round feed and the later 1865 variant. |
| Winchester 1887 | 1887 | Lever-action repeating shotgun. [Winchester's manufacturer timeline](https://www.winchesterguns.com/news/historical-timeline/1860-1899.html) explicitly records its 1887 introduction. Its five-shell game capacity and slow replenishment distinguish it from the double barrel. |

## Implementation choices

Each weapon has an original native Godot model and an identifiable action silhouette: flintlock, muzzle flare, rotating barrel cluster, open or solid revolver frame, break-open barrels, hinged breech, falling block, lever action or crossbow limbs. Animated action groups and staged reloads communicate these differences. The armory's lit 3D inspection view displays the same models used during play, with the first-person hands hidden.

Every trigger press produces at most one shot. The player does not receive a modern automatic weapon. Some historical actions are manually cocked or cycled, even though the game combines those actions with the firing animation for usability. Finite reserves, meaningful reload time, range limits, recoil and expensive upgrades preserve the difficult survival premise. The crossbow uses visible projectiles with travel time and drop. The LeMat's central shot barrel has its own ammunition state.

References checked 19 September 2026. No source artwork, photographs, 3D models or recordings were downloaded for the new armory.
